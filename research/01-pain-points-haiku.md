## 1. How notices reach Indian students

- WhatsApp is the default channel, and colleges use it officially. A J&K department notice tells first-semester BBA students to join a departmental WhatsApp group for "important announcements, academic updates, and official communications" [snippet: https://gdcboysang.ac.in/uploads/dept-notice/Notice-for-BBA-1st--Semester--Batch--2026--students-to-Join-the-Departmental-Official-WhatsApp-Group---1783403244.pdf]
- Institutes are moving away from WhatsApp for official notices:
  - APSIT Mumbai, IT HOD, posted 23 Sep 2025: "many students miss important notices and announcements" shared in WhatsApp groups. Replaced with an E-Notice Board; "will no longer share notices or announcements in individual WhatsApp groups." [verified: https://elearn.apsit.edu.in/moodle/mod/forum/discuss.php?d=65]
  - Jamia Millia Islamia, notice 7 Sep 2026 (article 9 Sep 2026): official class groups run by the class representative, each with a nominated faculty member who monitors. Students told not to use unofficial class groups for academic information. Article says unofficial groups "have become a source of contention" and were used for personal issues and obscene content. [verified: https://news.careers360.com/jamia-asks-students-deans-hod-to-avoid-unofficial-class-groups-jmi-channel]
- Telegram: used for test prep and study material. Factor Daily (28 Jan 2019) cites about 40 million Indian users [verified: https://archive.factordaily.com/telegram-turns-go-to-platform-for-test-prep-in-india-but-has-a-piracy-problem/]. This is a 2019 figure. No survey found on Telegram for official college notices.
- WhatsApp user numbers: sites quote about 535.8M Indian WhatsApp users from DataReportal Digital 2025. I did NOT open DataReportal. [unverified]. Recommend checking https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-india directly before citing any number.
- Google Classroom: no Indian student survey found. Non-India studies exist (Bangladesh, Nigeria, Philippines, Indonesia). A DESIDOC Journal study (2021, 1050 educators) is about teachers, not notices. Found via a search result, not opened [unverified]; the URL in that result was a different DRDO article, so do not cite it without checking.
- ERP: Haryana Higher Education memo (snippet, PDF text would not extract): not all colleges use ERP modules or the mobile app, and many do not update ERP data. [snippet: https://www.highereduhry.ac.in/Notices/1121.pdf, memo year 2019 per PDF metadata; content unverified]

Survey stat on WhatsApp in Indian education: one small Indian study (Karnataka State Women's University, postgraduate students) is described as "used quite regularly" for education. No percentage found. [unverified]

## 5. Edge cases from real notices

Real phrasing found in search snippets (pages not opened unless marked):
- Time change of a noon slot: Patna University (Jan 2025) moved PG/LLB exams from 12:00 noon-3 PM to 2-5 PM, same date and centre. [snippet only: a pup.ac.in "Revised Schedule ... 09.01.2025" PDF in search results; not opened, cite cautiously]
- Shift of start from 11 am to 12 noon, with a Friday exception: Kashmir University (Jan 2021). [snippet: https://kashmirreader.com/2021/01/08/kashmir-university-changes-exam-timing-of-ug-exams-except-on-fridays/]
- Postponement with same timings on revised dates: JNTUK (Feb 2019), SNDT (2020, "revised timetables will be posted on the university website later"). [snippet]
- Postponed mid-term timetable as a separate PDF: NSRIT "POSTPONED MID I TIME TABLE" [snippet]

Not found or not searched: "tentative", "same as above", multi-day events, recurring lab slots, dates without year, DD/MM, IST wording. No real examples gathered. Treat these as design assumptions to test with real notices from the user, not as facts.

Key pattern: the revised notice often gives only the changed part (time or date) and points to a website for the full timetable. A parser must link a revision to the original event, not create a second event.

## Top 5 insights that should shape the product (ranked by evidence strength)

1. Official notices get lost in WhatsApp, and institutes are moving to official channels. Strongest evidence: APSIT (Sep 2025) and Jamia (Sep 2026), both official. The product must accept WhatsApp paste as input, but users must be able to confirm which group or source a notice came from. Mark source as unverified when pasted.
2. Revisions are the norm: time shifts (noon to 2 pm), date moves, postponements, often with the full timetable on another site. Strong, but based on snippets. Revision handling (update existing event, show old vs new) is core, not an edge case.
3. Clashes happen with holidays and other exams, not just between two college notices (St Agnes Oct 2026, Bangalore Univ vs CA exam, Eid holiday cases). Clash check should include holidays and personal entrance or professional exams the user adds. Medium evidence (news, not measured).
4. Groups are seen as both burden and necessary (Baishya & Maheshwari 2020). Do not promise to reduce noise by default. Make the parser show only dated items, and let the user drop the rest. Medium (small study, country not stated).
5. Calendar adoption in India is unknown. Ship .ics export as the neutral bridge; do not claim student calendar habits. Lowest evidence. Verify with 5 to 10 real students before design decisions depend on it.
